# Jobs and Matching (spec I)

Part of the GawaGo spec; [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) has the rules most changes need and the section index.

## I. Employment Matching and Job Allocation

### A. Job Posting and Application
- Households can post job requests including:
  - Job type
  - Schedule, plus an optional job start date, end date, and estimated number of working days. Both sides see them the same way on My Jobs and the household dashboard: a Start and an End with the day of the week ("Fri, Oct 30, 2026"), worded Starts/Started and Ends/Ended, and the working days under them; a one-day job with no end date ends the day it starts (panel review, 2026-10-31). The worker's My Applications shows the same start and end as short lines in its Schedule column, with the schedule type beside the working days
  - Location
  - Service rate
  - A geographic search radius for matching (`1 km`, `2 km`, `5 km`, `10 km`, or `unlimited`; defaults to `1 km`)
- Since 2026-11-05 the Post a Job form asks for neither the working days nor the radius. The dates alone set how long a job runs, and the form says so under them ("5 days, Nov 10 to Nov 14, 2026." or "One-day job on ..."); every new job starts at `1 km`, and the household widens it from Edit Job when too few workers match. Edit Job no longer asks for working days either: a job posted earlier with a count keeps it until its dates are changed to include an end date, and then drops it.
- Workers can apply to available jobs based on:
  - Skills (a worker may list at most 3 skills on their profile)
  - Availability
- Households can review applicant profiles before confirming a worker.
- Application status is shown as a stage the backend owns (`apps/jobs/statuses.py`, sent as `status_stage`/`status_label`), so web and mobile always use the same words: `Pending` (applied, awaiting a decision), `Awaiting worker response` (the household invited a worker who has not answered), `In progress` (hired), `Completed` (the household marked the hired worker's job done), `Rejected`, `Closed` (the job filled or finished without this applicant), `Withdrawn` (the worker stepped away) and `Cancelled` (the job was cancelled). A worker who applied is hired as soon as the household accepts; only an invited worker has to confirm first. Only the household marks a job done: once a worker is hired the household gets "Mark as done" (the backend's `can_mark_done`, with `mark_done_note` saying why when it isn't available yet), the job becomes `Completed`, and only the hired worker's application follows it to `Completed`, with the worker notified. Workers do not mark jobs done (panel review, 2026-10-31); the old worker request endpoint now refuses with a message, and a job left `Awaiting confirmation` by that older flow can still be marked done by its household. Marking done is not allowed before the job's start date or by an unverified household, so a review always follows work that could have happened; anyone still pending reads `Closed`. Cancelling a job likewise closes every application still undecided. Cancelling a job that has a hired worker needs a reason (`cancel_reason`); the worker's notice names the household by display name and quotes it (third scan, 2026-11-01). The household can cancel at any point until the job is done, hired worker or not: `can_cancel` says when, and `cancel_needs_reason` when a reason is owed (fourth scan, 2026-11-01). The worker can step away too (`POST /jobs/applications/<id>/withdraw/`): a pending application is withdrawn (`can_withdraw`), and a hire can be given up with a reason until the job's first day (`can_back_out`); a job left with nobody hired opens again, and the household is told either way. A worker who withdrew can apply again while the job takes applicants. The day after a hired job's last day, its household is reminded once to mark it done or cancel it. Until it does, and until it has rated every worker on its finished jobs with a real review (the automatic 3.5 placeholder doesn't count), it can't post a new job: `POST /jobs/` answers 403 with `block_kind` (`mark_done` or `rate`) and `blocked_job_id`, and `GET /jobs/posting-status/` lets both clients show why on Post a Job, with a way to that job. A job still running, or not yet started, blocks nothing (2026-11-02).
- Hired jobs hold the worker (panel review, 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-01). The schedule type is One-Time, Part-Time or Full-Time on web and mobile. Any hired job holds the worker from other jobs on its own days, since jobs have dates but no hours. Once a Full-Time job has started it holds them from every job; before its first day it only holds its own days, so a Full-Time job booked for next month doesn't keep them from a one-day job this week (fourth scan, 2026-11-01). Every hold ends 3 days after the job's last day (`HOLD_GRACE_DAYS`) even if the household never marks it done. A held worker still sees open jobs, but the Apply button reads "Not available" and the backend's `apply_blocked_reason` says which job holds them and from when they can apply again. Other households can't invite or hire them either, and they drop out of recommended workers and job-match alerts meanwhile. Being hired closes the worker's other pending applications and hire requests that the hire rules out (all of them for a Full-Time job that has started); each of those households gets an "Application closed" notice that doesn't name the hiring household.
- One worker, one job per day (panel review, 2026-11-01). A worker hired for a job that is still under way can't take another job sharing any of its days, whatever the job type, since jobs have dates but no hours. A job's days run from its start date to its end date; with no end date they run for its working days (one day when unset), and a job with no start date isn't checked. Clashing jobs still list for the worker with "Not available" and the backend's `apply_blocked_title` ("Already hired on these days.") and `apply_blocked_reason`, which names the worker's own job, its household and its days. Other households get "…is already hired for another job on <days>, so they can't be invited or hired for these days" without the other household's name, and the worker leaves their recommended workers and job-match alerts for that job. Being hired closes the worker's pending applications and hire requests on the same days, and each of those households gets "Application closed". Jobs hired on the same days before this rule existed stay as they are.
- A job needing several workers stays in the market until every opening is filled (2026-11-01). After its first hire it moves to in progress but keeps taking applicants: the job list's `accepting=true` filter and the AI job recommendations include it, and every job carries `accepting_applications`. Workers' Find Jobs (web and mobile, list and map) lists jobs they can apply to, jobs another job of theirs holds them from, and jobs they applied to and are still waiting on, marked "Applied". On the household dashboard an in-progress job with openings shows "In progress · 1 of 3 hired · 1 applicant waiting" and View Applicants next to Mark as done.
- A job whose last day has passed takes no new applicants or hires (system scan, 2026-11-01). The last day is the end date, or the start date plus its working days. It leaves Find Jobs, recommendations and the `accepting=true` list; applying, inviting and hiring are refused with the dates. An open job that lapses with nobody hired carries `expired_note` for its household ("Start date passed - nobody was hired" on the card), and editing the dates puts it back. On every household card a multi-worker job lists all its hired workers. When the household marks a job done, anyone still waiting is told "Job closed: <household> marked <job> as done", not that another worker completed it. Find Jobs counts only the jobs the worker can still apply to ("5 jobs you can apply to · 2 already applied").
- AI job recommendations leave out jobs the worker is already hired on (a multi-worker job stays in the market after a hire). Chat between a household and a hired worker opens while the job is in progress; once it is done or cancelled the thread stays readable (`read_only`) and only sending stops. The home page reads its totals from the public `GET /analytics/public-summary/` (counts only), since signed-out visitors can't load profiles (second scan, 2026-11-01).

### B. Smart Matching with Distance Indicator
- System ranks and suggests workers using, in priority order:
  1. Skill matching: workers with matching skills appear first.
  2. Verification status: verified workers are prioritized.
  3. Distance: workers are ranked by road-route distance (not straight-line), and any worker outside the job's geographic search radius is excluded outright rather than merely ranked lower.
  4. Rating and offered rate as tie-breakers.
- Distance is shown to households as a road-route estimate from the worker's registered location to the job site (for example, `2 km away`).
- Households retain final hiring choice regardless of ranking (can still choose based on price, ratings, experience, or preference).

### C. AI-Based Job Recommendations
- Separate from Smart Matching (which ranks *workers for a household's job*), this feature ranks *open jobs for a worker* using a content-based recommendation algorithm rather than the fixed rule ordering above.
- Scoring: each skill a worker lists is compared with the job's required skill using TF-IDF cosine similarity, and the best fit is the skill-similarity score (comparing the whole list as one document diluted an exact match with every other skill listed; fourth scan, 2026-11-01); a road-route proximity score (`1 / (1 + distance_km)`) is computed the same way Smart Matching computes distance. The final score blends the two as `0.7 × skill similarity + 0.3 × proximity`, so a skill mismatch outweighs being nearby. Jobs outside their own configured search radius are excluded before scoring, the same hard-boundary rule Smart Matching applies.
- Pull delivery: a worker can request "Find Jobs for Me" to see their open jobs ranked by this score, each shown with a `% match` badge and distance.
- Push delivery: when a household posts a new open job, the system scores it against every candidate worker in the background and sends a job-match notification to any worker scoring above a configurable threshold (`JOB_MATCH_NOTIFY_THRESHOLD`), or with an exact skill match, instead of waiting for the worker to check manually. The job-create request only saves the job; see "Background tasks" under Implementation Notes for how the pass runs.
- Household delivery: the same content-based score also runs in the opposite direction, ranking *workers for a job*. Immediately after a household posts a job they land on a recommended-workers screen showing each candidate's `% match` and the same skill-versus-proximity breakdown a worker sees. This does not replace Smart Matching: the job detail screen still lists candidates in Smart Matching's rule order (which prioritises verification), and both scores are returned on every result so the two remain complementary rather than competing.
- Explainability: each recommendation also reports how much of the score came from skill fit versus proximity (and whether the match was an exact skill match, not just a related one), shown to the worker as a breakdown (for example, "70% skill fit, 30% proximity") rather than a single opaque number.
- Future enhancement: once enough completed-job/hire outcome data exists, this can be extended with a collaborative-filtering signal ("workers with similar hire histories were chosen for jobs like this") layered on top of the content-based score. That requires a worker-job interaction dataset that does not yet exist, so it is out of scope for now.
